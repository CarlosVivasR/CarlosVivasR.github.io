Outside the lab I apply the same habits to football: start from a clear question, test models on data they have never seen, and report what didn't work as plainly as what did. How I work

Validate out of sample

A model only counts if it predicts matches it was not fitted on. Every model here is backtested walk-forward, with no information from the future leaking into the past.

Report the negatives

When a model loses to the bookmaker, or a signal turns out weak, that goes on the page too. Knowing where a method fails is what makes it usable.

Respect the data

Open data for anything I model and publish; licensed or scraped data only for private analysis. Every source is credited.

Match prediction · June 2026

World Cup 2026 match model

Question
How well can a model built only on past international results predict World Cup matches, and does it add anything to the bookmakers' odds?
Why
The prediction pool I built for friends first just converted betting odds into scorelines. It added no information of its own, had never been tested, and went silent for knockout fixtures that had no odds yet.
Method
Dixon-Coles Poisson model with time decay (four-year half-life) and down-weighted friendlies; Elo ratings as a coverage layer; blended with de-vigged odds through a logarithmic opinion pool. Walk-forward backtest with no leakage, paired bootstrap tests, and a bake-off against gradient boosting, a neural network and an ordered logit.
Data
About 49,000 international results (martj42, CC0) and international 1X2 odds from football-data.co.uk for the head-to-head against the market.
Result
ComparisonRPS ↓Matches
Dixon-Coles backtest0.1684,007
Climatology baseline (home/draw/away rates)~0.228—
Major tournaments: Dixon-Coles vs Elo0.179 vs 0.186420

On historical matches the market was slightly but consistently better (0.006 RPS, 95% CI 0.003–0.009), so in the app the model became one input next to the odds, not the oracle. An earlier version of this comparison showed a far larger gap; cleaning up the code for publication exposed a sorting bug that paired forecasts with the wrong matches. At the World Cup itself, scored out of sample with the ratings that were live before each match day, it was level with the bookmakers (difference inside the 95% bootstrap interval), ahead of an Elo baseline (0.162) and far ahead of base rates (0.226). It called 65% of 90-minute results. With 104 matches, “level with the market” is the claim the data supports, not “better”.
Status
Ran live through the tournament (June–July 2026); evaluated on all 104 matches in October 2026.

Player profiling · April 2026

Beyond Goals & Assists: La Liga 2025-26

Question
Who creates danger in La Liga without getting the credit in goals and assists?
Why
Goals and assists are what everyone sees, but much of a midfielder's or forward's work is in key passes, chances created, dribbles and defensive actions.
Method
Per-90 metrics, percentile-ranked. A Creative Score combining 11 metrics is set against Visibility (the goals-plus-assists percentile); the gap is the player's “hidden value”. Equivalent scores for defenders and goalkeepers, plus passing networks and shot maps. An offline extension groups players into six archetypes with k-means and finds similar profiles.
Data
Season statistics from FBref (powered by StatsBomb) and Sofascore; StatsBomb Open Data for 294 historical La Liga matches in the match-analysis tab.
Result
An interactive six-tab dashboard covering 192 midfielders and forwards with at least eight full matches. Limits: season aggregates only (no 2025-26 event data), score weights are a judgement call, and it covers one league.
Status
Published April 2026.

Injury risk · May 2026

Overuse injuries in summer tournaments

Question
Which workload, injury-history and context patterns separate players who suffer a serious overuse injury around a summer international tournament?
Why
Summer tournaments come at the end of a long club season, and “too many minutes” is the usual explanation for injuries. I wanted to see whether the data supports it.
Method
Cox proportional-hazards survival models on player-tournament entries across 15 tournaments, with severe overuse injury (29+ days out) from a month before to two weeks after the tournament as the outcome.
Data
Open tournament and squad datasets (Fjelstul World Cup database, openfootball, transfermarkt-datasets) and injury records collected from Transfermarkt.
Result
Validated by leaving one tournament out at a time: C-index 0.63 (95% CI 0.55–0.71), against 0.66 in-sample. Past injury recurrence carries a modest signal; club minutes carry none (hazard ratio 1.00). The apparent “goalkeepers are protected” effect largely disappears once injury history is taken into account. With 75 events, this is an honest weak-signal result, not a risk tool.

Product · May–July 2026

World Cup 2026 prediction pool

What
A web app where 18 friends and family predicted the whole tournament, with live results, a scoring engine, team and player pages in Spanish, English and Turkish, and the match model above competing as one more participant.
Why
To put the model in front of real users and see it hold up day to day, not just in a backtest.
Lesson
Validate inputs where they are entered: a bracket rule gap let 16 of 18 players pick a final that could not happen, which had to be fixed by re-scoring afterwards.
Status
Finished with the tournament; kept private for its participants.
