England 1-1 Spain: post-match report

UEFA Women's Euro 2025 final · 27 July 2025, St. Jakob-Park · England win 3-1 on penalties · Data: StatsBomb Open Data

A one-page team report written for England's staff: how the game was played, where each side had the ball and won it back, set pieces, and what to take into the next training week.

Why this match

It is the final of the most recent women's tournament in StatsBomb Open Data, and the result and the performance point in opposite directions. That is the situation where a post-match report earns its keep: a staff that only looks at the scoreline learns the wrong lessons, in either direction.

Pass networks

  • England completed 238 passes in this window and the strongest link was Greenwood–Carter, 20 passes. Most of the circulation stayed with the back four and the two holding midfielders.
  • Spain completed 424. Their busiest link (Bonmatí–Batlle, 40 passes) was down England's left side.

Territory and pressing

  • England made 40% of their 539 defensive actions in their own third; Spain made 30% of theirs in the attacking third.
  • Spain regained the ball in the attacking third 25 times, and 5 of those were followed by a Spain shot within 15 seconds (England: 16 and 4).

Key takeaways for the coaching staff

  1. We won the final, not the match. Spain created 2.14 xG from 23 shots against our 0.88 from 8. Replaying every shot as a coin flip with its xG gives Spain 69%, a draw 19% and England 12% over 120 minutes. Take the trophy, not the tactical lessons, from the score.
  2. No out-ball once Spain settled. Our field tilt was 20% over the match and 10% in extra time (97 final-third passes), with no shots in extra time against Spain's 5 (0.70 xG). A PPDA of 20.5 says we sat off. Training focus: a rehearsed release pattern for protecting a draw, so the block can step up.
  3. The goal came from wide delivery. Russo's header (57', 0.21 xG) came from a Chloe Kelly cross, in a possession that started from a throw-in. Our best spell for territory was 75-90' (52% field tilt). Keep a crossing threat on the pitch when we need a goal.
  4. Check our left side on video. Spain's busiest link (Bonmatí–Batlle, 40 passes) ran down our left, and their goal (25', 0.40 xG) was a header from Batlle's cross on that side. Meanwhile 45% of our defensive actions were on our right. That is a hypothesis for the video session, not a conclusion: event data cannot show who should have tracked whom.
  5. Set-piece defending held, but conceded volume. Conceding 7 shots and 0.84 xG from corners and free kicks without a goal is partly luck. Our own set-piece threat was absent.

Method and caveats

  • One match. Every number here describes this game only. A 120-minute final with a lead to protect is an unusual game state, so do not treat these as team tendencies.
  • xG is StatsBomb's model. The replay treats shots as independent, which overstates variance for rebounds in the same attack.
  • Event data only. No tracking data, so pressing is measured by on-ball defensive actions, and pass-network positions are averages that mix build-up and defending phases.
